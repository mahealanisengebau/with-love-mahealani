Friends are the Family You Choose

To the people who have shined a light for me in my darkest moments, and to the people I have chased waterfalls, sunsets, oceans, and more with - this one’s for you.

The bond we have and the family we’ve created is something that I take big pride in. I could talk about you for hours and could write pages about all our adventures. We have loved each other through every awkward stage, every heartbreak, every failed relationship, and every ghost phase, and now, a majority of us have children of our own.

A big thing I have been reflecting on this year is the fact that every person you come across on Earth serves a purpose. You all are living proof of that. I am not going to name everyone individually because this section will get rather lengthy, but if you guys do take anything from this part of the site, it’s that I wouldn’t be me without you. One thing I pray about often is how grateful I am that only the seas separate us (and not death). One of the main reasons I keep going is our vision for us to all be together again, with the new generation: our children.

I am an Amalgamation of All My Experiences

I wouldn’t call myself religious, but I can confidently say I am spiritual.

I owe my relationship with Jesus to my Dad’s sisters and my two Grandmothers. From a young age, they took me to church, as well as modeled for me what a servant of Jesus looks like. By watching them, I learned to pray, and I have always looked up to their faith. It’s a privilege to have strong, God-fearing women to look up to.

I’m not the perfect Christian, and I never claim to be, but I take pride in the fact that Jesus would never leave me no matter how far I stray. I had Jesus planted in my heart from a young age, and it’s the greatest blessing. My faith is strong, and all that I am is because of Him. In my darkest moments, He was the One Constant that never left me.

I read a tweet that said,

“You can inherit language for God from other people, but you cannot inherit intimacy with God. At some point, what you were taught has to become something you’ve actually experienced and chosen for yourself”.

As a young teen, when things got too hostile at home, I used to run away to Father McManus Church and lay down on the second floor and look at the stars. That was when I first felt closest to God. The sense of comfort I felt just being in His home has stayed with me through every era I’ve been in, and it’s one of the core memories I look back on today when I find myself back in the dark places.

My faith is not something I talk about often because I don’t feel like I have the right to talk about it when I don’t serve or go to church often, but I do my best to plant the seeds in my daughter by praying with her every night before bed. It gets repetitive sometimes, and some nights it’s almost robotic, but I just hope that I am going about it the correct way. I don’t call myself religious because I don’t really see God the way they paint Him in Church as a Man in the sky. I see God in the trees when I look up at the evergreens that surround my house; I see Jesus’ love in my daughter; I see God’s angels visiting me when the black and white butterflies pass by, and I see God in my friends’ love for me. “Love is patient and kind” is something that I truly learned recently, and something that I do my best to live by every day.
